# Rivals Hand-Build What Unitforma Automates

Yes, plenty of products overlap with Unitforma, in Georgia and worldwide. None of the ones found combines its pieces, and none automatically turns a developer's drawing set into a verified unit inventory. In Tbilisi a developer today puts together several vendors: a chooser website built by a local agency (Prostor starts at **$2,500**), a back office on Bitrix24, Odoo or a small local CRM, render and 3D-tour studios, and listings on myhome.ge, ss.ge and Korter. The most direct threats come from abroad. **DomusHub** markets a site → building → floor → unit master plan with GEL prices and instalment plans to Georgian developers. **WIZIO** says it will launch a Georgian interactive off-plan marketplace in early 2027. The Ukrainian vendors **Smart Orange** and **G-PLUS** claim Georgian clients, and the Russian vendor **Profitbase** runs a live chessboard for one Batumi developer. Worldwide the category is split in two. CIS "chessboard" CRMs and Western inventory CRMs (Spark) hold the inventory. Visual choosers are either built per project by studios (**$5,700–$20,000**) or assembled in self-serve 2D tools (Planpoint, from about **$69/month**). Unitforma's defensible edge is its drawings-to-inventory pipeline, its tas.ge permit import and an MCP server built on the developer's own inventory. Its AI renders and renovation playground are commodities on their own. The strategy that follows: position as the inventory source of truth plus an auto-generated chooser that replaces the agency fee, sync with the CRMs developers already run, feed the portals rather than fight them, and recruit agencies and studios as partners.

> **Confidence caveat.** The session's network policy blocked full-page fetches of almost every vendor site; only tas.ge could be opened directly. Nearly every finding here therefore comes from search-engine snippets of the cited pages, much of it vendor marketing, sponsored articles or third-party software directories with stale or conflicting prices. Statements of the form "no competitor does X" mean "no competitor was found doing X in a snippet-level search". They are absence of evidence, not proof. The vendor behind each major Tbilisi developer's website (Archi, m2, Alliance, Next and others) could not be identified, because their sites could not be fetched.

## Georgia's status quo is agencies, portals and imported CRMs

No Georgian startup was found that automates drawings → inventory → branded chooser. Large Tbilisi developers build their own sites. Archi, which calls itself the country's #1 developer with an 18% market share ([Breaking Travel News](https://www.breakingtravelnews.com/news/article/georgia-strengthens-investment-links-with-gulf-markets-at-2026-internationa/)), runs a "Find your home" list on each complex page. It filters by floor, area, rooms and price, lists units by block, and ends in "Request a call" rather than online reservation ([Archi Universe](https://archi.ge/en/complex/archi-universe)). Smaller developers commission agencies. **Prostor**, a roughly 12-person Tbilisi studio whose "most clients are real estate developers", builds sites with a chessboard by block and floor, unit pages with PDF plans, live CRM statuses and up to four languages, **from $2,500** ([Prostor](https://en.prostor-agency.com/georgia/real-estate); [GoodFirms](https://www.goodfirms.co/company/prostor)). The cheapest route is a WordPress image-map plugin, in which someone hand-draws clickable SVG polygons over renders; the free tier allows one project and 25 flats ([WordPress.org](https://wordpress.org/plugins/interactive-real-estate/)). All three routes involve the manual polygon and inventory work that Unitforma automates.

The back office is just as fragmented. **NK Solutions**, a Tbilisi operator, sells a Georgian-language sales CRM with a colour-coded unit board, payment plans and contracts at a fixed project price ([nksolutions.ge](https://nksolutions.ge/en/product)). **FMG Soft** has localised Odoo for development companies with an apartment and parking module, and Property Georgia calls it "a leader in digitalisation of development companies in Georgia" ([Property Georgia](https://propertygeorgia.ge/en/media/news/1790-fmg-soft-leader-digitalisation-development-companies-georgia)). Bitrix24 shows up through local integrators such as BSMGROUP ([BSMGROUP](https://en.bsmgroup.ge/services/bitrix24-danergva/)) and in a Tbilisi CRM-manager job ad ([cv.ge](https://www.cv.ge/en/announcement/480940/CRM-Manager)). No Georgian developer was found naming its CRM publicly, so the claim that Bitrix24 and Kommo/amoCRM are the incumbents is an inference to test in pilot interviews.

Portals are lead channels, not white-label tools. myhome.ge (TNET) is Georgia's #1 real-estate site, ss.ge is #2 and korter.ge is #3 at about 140K visits, per Semrush third-party estimates ([Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/trending-websites/ge/real-estate)). Korter lists projects with floor plans, prices and construction updates. An older article says "everything is placed free of charge" for developers ([cbw.ge](https://cbw.ge/real-estate/korter-reliable-information-on-residential-real-estate-in-georgia)), and a 2026 release added price-trend analytics and AI for listing quality ([EZ Newswire](https://www.eznewswire.com/newsroom/korter-georgia-residential-real-estate-transparency-construction-tracking)). The portal to watch is **livo.ge**, a TNET + TBC platform that already bundles valuation, **3D scanning** and an escrow-style secure transaction system ([Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/website/myhome.ge/competitors/); [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/madona-kachkachashvili/)). It is the local player best placed to move into developer tooling.

The inference is that roughly 200 small and mid-size developers without in-house digital teams are Unitforma's realistic SaaS segment. Top developers such as Archi already have bespoke sites. Pressure is rising on the rest: TBC Capital warns that potential supply keeps growing and intensifies competition ([internationalinvestment.biz](https://internationalinvestment.biz/en/real-estate/6570-colliers-forecast-georgias-housing-prices-to-remain-stable-in-2026.html)).

## The CIS commoditised the chessboard but still draws choosers by hand

The Russian-speaking market has the most mature version of Unitforma's inventory layer. **Profitbase** is the de facto chessboard that plugs into Bitrix24 and amoCRM, costing about **2,900 RUB per user per month** for the CRM app ([Bitrix24](https://www.bitrix24.ru/apps/app/profitbase.crm/)) and roughly 15,000 RUB per month per domain for its website "smart catalog" ([crmindex](https://crmindex.ru/products/profitbase)). Its AI is about pricing, not drawings: Profitbase.ai offers dynamic pricing and "AI pricing of the chessboard" ([1cbit.kz](https://www.1cbit.kz/1csoft/profitbase-dlya-nedvizhimosti/)). **MacroCRM** claims 200+ developers ([MACRO presentation](https://profi.erzrf.ru/upload/iblock/466/9kwyyjzdhhwefmiwvj27x1z01741gwpm/MACRO-Nigmatulin-TSifrovaya-evolyutsiya-developmenta.pdf)) and starts at 12,500 RUB per month for five users ([a2is.ru](https://a2is.ru/catalog/programmy-dlya-stroitelstva-i-remonta/macro-crm)). **Domoplaner** sells a "chessboard for your website, for any CRM" for 10,000 RUB per month ([crmindex](https://crmindex.ru/products/domoplaner)), and Plan7, DomXML and Ained fill the Bitrix24 marketplace with similar apps ([Bitrix24 collection](https://www.bitrix24.ru/apps/collection/19275292/)). In Kazakhstan, Amanix Build models developer → complex → building → floor → apartment around a colour-coded grid ([dev.to, promotional](https://dev.to/tech_insider_kz/the-first-native-crm-for-kazakhstan-property-developers-how-amanix-build-works-j99)). Further west, Prague's **Realpad** reports 150+ developer customers in 12 countries for CRM, sales app and handover ([property-forum.eu](https://www.property-forum.eu/news/czech-proptech-brings-digital-solutions-to-residential-developers-in-romania/8196); [startupjobs.cz](https://www.startupjobs.cz/nabidka/80291/customer-success-manager-at-realpad)).

These vendors share a limitation. Staff or a partner agency enter the inventory and draw the visuals. Profitbase's widget exports the site plan and statuses automatically, but the site plan is first laid out inside Profitbase by the developer or an "official partner" agency ([workspace.ru case](https://workspace.ru/cases/kamastroyinvest-razrabotka-sayta-zastroyschika-s-vnedreniem-profitbase/)), and one user calls drawing site plans with it "not cheap at all" ([Habr Q&A](https://qna.habr.com/q/566249)). The closest regional automation is Poland's **3D Estate**, which generates 3D tours automatically from apartment data sheets ([bank.pl](https://bank.pl/?p=399408)), and Getfloorplan's AI plan-to-3D conversion. Both produce visuals, not inventory. The chessboard is a solved, cheap commodity. Getting drawings into the chessboard without retyping them is the problem nobody in the region claims to have solved.

The geopolitical angle needs verification. Russian vendors price in RUB and build around Russian accounting, mortgage and registration flows; Profitbase's deal workspace includes Rosreestr state registration ([1cbit.kz](https://www.1cbit.kz/1csoft/profitbase-dlya-nedvizhimosti/)). No Profitbase, MacroCRM, Domoplaner or Allio clients were found in Tbilisi or Armenia. That Russian SaaS faces payment, data-residency or reputational friction in Georgia, and that Ukrainian vendors have taken the opening, is a plausible but unsourced inference.

## Western leaders split inventory, visuals and design models across vendors

Outside the CIS, no single product spans Unitforma's scope. **Spark** (Vancouver), the leading new-development CRM, has a live pricing matrix, allocations, options and multi-building projects ([Spark inventory](https://www.spark.re/product/inventory/)), but its partner page sends the interactive selector to **Planpoint**, which pulls availability and pricing from Spark ([Spark partners](https://www.spark.re/partner-integrations)). Planpoint is the closest self-serve analogue: a no-code editor that "builds interactive stacking plans without coding", embeddable in WordPress, Wix and Webflow, with views from 2D up to a 3D building ([SourceForge](https://sourceforge.net/software/product/Planpoint-Viewer/)), priced from **USD 69/month by units actively marketed** ([Software Advice AU](https://www.softwareadvice.com.au/software/558552/Planpoint)). No source shows that it ingests CAD or extracts units automatically. Spark's head-to-head comparison page against **Avesdo**, a Toronto transaction-management platform, confirms that the CRM layer is a contest of its own ([spark.re](https://www.spark.re/compare/spark-vs-avesdo)).

Three other models stand out. **gBuilder** (Finland) is the only buyer-facing product built on BIM: "the whole system and also the 3D models are based on DWG or IFC files" ([gBuilder](https://gbuilder.com/resources/gbuilder-revolutionises-off-plan-homes-3d-visualisations)). It powers YIT's 400+ unit Helsinki project ([gBuilder/YIT](https://gbuilder.com/customer-stories/yit)), but it concentrates on finish selection and upsell, and most sources date from 2018–2022. **Higharc** shows where the money is going. It replaces AutoCAD for US single-family builders, uses "AutoTranslate AI" to turn plan images and sketches into a 3D model ([AEC Magazine](https://aecmag.com/bim/higharc-ai-3d-bim-model-from-2d-sketch/)), and generates a buyer showroom with live pricing ([Higharc Showroom](https://higharc.com/product/showroom)). It raised a **$95M Series C** in June 2026 ([Insight Partners](https://www.insightpartners.com/ideas/higharc-raises-95m-series-c-to-scale-ai-for-homebuilding/)). **Studios** fill the rest of the market: Maverick Frame sells building → floor → apartment selectors in eight languages including Turkish, but "you send the architect's plans. We build the 3D model", and "every option needs its own render" ([Maverick Frame](https://maverickframe.com/services/interactive-apartment-selector/)). Buildmedia (NZ), PropVR (Dubai) and Bayut Studios do the same at the high end ([Unreal Engine](https://www.unrealengine.com/spotlights/propvrs-digital-twins-help-real-estate-developer-boost-revenue-by-10x); [Entrepreneur ME](https://www.entrepreneur.com/en-ae/technology/bayut-expands-its-proptech-ecosystem-with-bayut-studios-a/499815)).

Deal activity points the same way. Selector tools are being absorbed by data, CRM and portal groups: Zonda bought Alpha Vision's site maps ([Zonda](https://zondahome.com/zonda-acquires-alpha-vision/)), Spark bought Juniper Homeowner Care ([BetaKit](https://betakit.com/spark-real-estate-acquires-juniper/)), Square Yards bought PropVR ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/square-yards-acquires-propvr?amp=1)), and CoStar completed its Matterport acquisition ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250228873241/en/CoStar-Group-Completes-Acquisition-of-Matterport-Ushering-in-a-New-Era-of-3D-Digital-Twins-and-AI-Powered-Real-Estate-Innovation)). Studios that only build selectors are not raising notable venture money. The likely acquirers of a selector product are portals, data companies and CRM suites, which in Georgia means TNET/TBC (livo.ge) or Korter.

Emerging off-plan markets show which features decide a purchase. India's Sell.Do lets developers restrict channel partners to a subset of inventory and handle payment requests and KYC ([Sell.Do](https://www.sell.do/real-estate-crm/channel-partners)). UAE tools such as PropSpace Developer and Asas centre on off-plan payment plans and milestones ([PropSpace](https://www.propspace.com/blog/introducing-propspace-developer-sales); [Asas](https://asas.araneae.net/)). Georgia fits this pattern: **83% of apartments in Tbilisi projects due in 2025 were already sold, mostly via developers' internal instalment plans** ([bm.ge](https://bm.ge/en/news/83-of-apartments-in-tbilisi-projects-set-for-completion-in-2025-already-sold-gt)). DomusHub already leads with instalment plans for exactly that reason.

## Drawing automation is the moat; renders and 3D viewers are not

Taking Unitforma's features one at a time against the closest rival found separates the defensible claims from the table stakes.

| Unitforma capability | Closest rival found | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| PDF/DXF/IFC → human-verified unit inventory | Takeoff tools (Kreo, Togal) read rooms and areas for estimators; Higharc converts single-family plans; 3D Estate builds visuals from data sheets | **Unmatched** for multi-unit sales inventory |
| Branded site → building → floor → unit chooser | DomusHub, Planpoint, Profitbase widget, studios | Contested; the edge is that it is auto-generated |
| IFC model in the chooser | gBuilder (finishes focus); free viewers (Speckle, web-ifc) | Partly contested; IFC space ↔ unit linking is the edge |
| tas.ge permit import | None | **Unique in Georgia** |
| AI plan renders | Getfloorplan, Planner 5D B2B, FloorVis | **Commodity** |
| Renovation playground | RoomSketcher, Floorplanner, Roomle | Commodity unless tied to true-scale unit geometry |
| AI assistant / MCP server | EliseAI (rentals); listing-data MCPs (Zealty, Cotality) | **Novel** for developer inventory, but easy to copy |
| Embeddable chooser | Planpoint, Domoplaner, Profitbase | Table stakes |

The extraction pipeline is the real differentiator, and its defensibility comes from the specifics. Takeoff vendors come closest technically. Kreo 6.0 classifies PDF pages by drawing type and labels rooms with name and area ([Kreo](https://kreo.net/news-2d-takeoff/agentic-computer-vision-for-construction-drawings)), and Togal claims 98% area accuracy, a vendor figure ([Togal](https://www.togal.ai/blog/ai-blueprint-reading-accuracy)). Both sell to estimators and output no sellable inventory. Single-unit converters need one dwelling per file, and RoomSketcher makes the user set scale by hand ([RoomSketcher](https://help.roomsketcher.com/hc/en-us/articles/31885247016605)). Single-dwelling raster segmentation is close to solved in research; Raster2Seq reaches about 89 room-F1 on CubiCasa5K ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09016v1)). It is therefore not a moat. The harder, less-served problems are vector parsing of multi-apartment floor plates, Georgian labels, level markers, dimension-chain scaling, DXF zone and hatch conventions, and IFC space-to-unit linking. Open Georgian OCR is weak: community trainers call Tesseract's Georgian model "passable, but not excellent" ([tesseract-ocr group](https://groups.google.com/g/tesseract-ocr/c/R_-9cduyixc)). Frontier vision models still fall "significantly below human baseline" on spatial reasoning ([Startup Fortune](https://startupfortune.com/blueprint-bench-2-tests-whether-ai-models-can-actually-reason-about-3d-space-and-the-results-should-recalibrate-what-spatial-ai-startups-expect-from-frontier-apis/)), which supports a deterministic, vector-first pipeline with a person reviewing the result. The lasting moat is the workflow plus tenant-corrected data accumulated over time. Kreo's "1,000,000 new rooms" dataset ([Kreo](https://www.kreo.net/news-2d-takeoff/1-000-000-new-rooms-in-the-kreo-dataset)) shows how incumbents in adjacent markets build that kind of moat.

The weak flanks are just as clear. AI furnished renders are cheap and plentiful, and Getfloorplan already targets developers. It has converted 1M+ plans and is "testing AI personalization inside our 3D tour widget" ([LinkedIn](https://linkedin.com/company/getfloorplan)). BIM-to-web viewing is free. Speckle's open-source viewer opens IFC files up to 1 GB ([Speckle](https://speckle.systems/integrations/ifc/)). Real-estate MCP servers already exist for listing and market data, including Cotality's Claude connector ([eSEOspace](https://eseospace.com/blog/best-mcp-servers-for-real-estate/)), so the MCP edge is a short head start, not a barrier.

Four risks stand out. First, DomusHub already sells the chooser + GEL + instalments bundle to Georgian developers. If it adds drawing ingestion, the gap narrows to Georgian-language fit and tas.ge. Second, WIZIO's planned marketplace could set buyer expectations, and could sign developers to a buyer-side platform, before Unitforma's pilot scales. Third, livo.ge or Korter could add white-label tooling on top of the traffic they already have. Fourth, Unitforma's later-list items (instalment plans, reservations, broker portals with restricted inventory) are the features that decide purchases in instalment-driven markets like Tbilisi.

## Integrate with the CRMs, feed the portals, recruit the agencies

**Positioning.** Unitforma should sell itself as the developer's verified inventory source of truth and an auto-generated chooser: upload drawings, approve the extracted units, and publish in days without an agency or a polygon-drawing contractor. Lead with the hard part (verified inventory and areas from the real drawings) rather than with AI renders or 3D, which competitors and free tools already offer. The "human-verified" label is a trust asset in a market where Korter promotes staff verification as its selling point ([cbw.ge](https://cbw.ge/real-estate/korter-reliable-information-on-residential-real-estate-in-georgia)). The primary target is the long tail of small and mid-size Tbilisi developers. The largest developers are better approached for a single project or an embeddable widget for their existing sites.

**CRM integrations.** The regional pattern is a specialised inventory tool that syncs into a general CRM, which is exactly the slot Unitforma would occupy. **Bitrix24** comes first: its marketplace already hosts inventory connectors such as the Profitbase connector, which syncs units into deals every 15 minutes ([Bitrix24 app](https://www.bitrix24.ru/apps/app/metod_tsifry.konnektor_profitbase/)). **Kommo/amoCRM** is second, and **Odoo** (FMG Soft's localisation) third, followed by generic webhooks and Zapier/Make. A Profitbase-compatible export eases switching for Batumi developers, and HubSpot custom objects ([clck.com.au](https://www.clck.com.au/blog/hubspot-property-development-inventory/)) matter later in the West. Two-way status sync needs an explicit rule about which system owns the data, because CIS-style CRMs assume they own the inventory. API details for Bitrix24, Kommo and Odoo were not fetched and need checking against their developer docs.

**Portal feeds.** myhome.ge, ss.ge, korter.ge and livo.ge are where Georgian buyers search, so Unitforma should push verified inventory and prices to them rather than compete for traffic. Whether these portals accept developer feeds is unverified, and it is the first question for a partnership conversation. TNET/TBC and Korter are also the most plausible strategic acquirers.

**Partners.**
- **Web agencies** (Prostor and similar) as implementers and resellers. They keep the design work, and Unitforma removes their manual chooser build.
- **Visualisation and 3D-tour studios** (3D Tours Georgia, Paralel, ModX) as suppliers of renders and facades for the chooser ([3d-tours.ge](https://3d-tours.ge/); [Behance](https://www.behance.net/paralel)).
- **TwinO** as a possible partner for the AI sales assistant.
- **Unitify** for post-handover continuity.

**Licence-compatible components** (open source):
- **web-ifc** (MPL-2.0) and **IfcOpenShell** (LGPL) for IFC parsing ([IfcOpenShell](https://www.github.com/IfcOpenShell/IfcOpenShell)). Both comply with the repository's no-AGPL rule.
- **Speckle's viewer**, open source ([Speckle](https://speckle.systems/integrations/ifc/)).

**Commercial suppliers:**
- **Collov or Decor8** APIs for photo restyling ([Collov](https://collov.ai/features/api-for-virtual-staging-services)).
- **Planner 5D's recognition API** as a fallback for raster single-unit plans ([Planner 5D](https://tool.planner5d.com/b2b-floor-plan-recognition)).

**Near-term event.** Realty Forum Tbilisi Invest runs **17–19 November 2026** with a New Development Expo ([internationalinvestment.biz](https://internationalinvestment.biz/business/9049-16.html)). That is nine days after this report. WIZIO-type vendors recruit at events like this, so it is the obvious place to meet developers and check competitor claims.

## Conclusion

The landscape changes the question from "does anyone do this?" to "who else could do it first?". The market has settled into separate layers: CRM vendors hold the inventory, studios build the chooser, and portals hold the traffic. Developers pay three times and retype the same data. Unitforma's opportunity is to collapse those layers by getting the inventory straight from the drawings. Its realistic competitors are an all-in-one vendor already courting Georgia (DomusHub), a marketplace launching next year (WIZIO), and portals with traffic (livo.ge, Korter). Startups like Unitforma are unlikely to be the threat. In that race the defensible assets are local and procedural rather than technological: Georgian-language drawings, tas.ge, verified areas and a growing set of corrected floor plates. AI renders, 3D viewers and the MCP server help sell the product but will not protect it.

Three actions would most improve confidence in these findings. Allow the blocked hosts so that a session can fingerprint which vendor powers each major Tbilisi developer site and check DomusHub's and WIZIO's real Georgian traction. Interview pilot developers about their CRM, instalment workflow and current agency spend. Confirm whether the Georgian portals accept inventory feeds. Until then, read every "no one else does this" in this report as "no one else was found doing this".
